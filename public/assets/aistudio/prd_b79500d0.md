# AI Sales Strategist — Product Requirements Document (PRD)

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Draft for discovery and MVP planning  
**Product owner:** To be assigned  
**Initial release:** Simulated-meeting MVP, followed by one real meeting integration  
**Initial market:** B2B SaaS sales teams  
**Last updated:** 2026-11-17

---

## 1. Document purpose

This PRD defines the product scope, users, workflows, functional and non-functional requirements, data needs, success metrics, acceptance criteria, risks and release plan for AI Sales Strategist.

It is intended for product, design, engineering, QA, security and pilot stakeholders. It separates the first demonstrable MVP from later commercial releases.

The PRD is a working specification. Requirements should be updated after user research, prototype testing and technical validation.

---

## 2. Product overview

### 2.1 Product vision

Help every salesperson make better decisions during customer conversations by bringing relevant organisational knowledge and contextual guidance to the moment it is needed.

### 2.2 Product description

AI Sales Strategist is a proactive sales companion. It receives a permitted meeting transcript or audio-derived transcript, identifies meaningful conversation signals, retrieves relevant approved company knowledge and presents concise recommendations to a human sales representative.

### 2.3 Product principles

1. Context over keywords.
2. Evidence over unsupported generation.
3. Proactive but not intrusive.
4. Human remains in control.
5. Recommendations must be actionable.
6. Privacy and permissioning are foundational.
7. Abstention is preferable to an unsupported answer.

### 2.4 Initial product positioning

A real-time sales intelligence and coaching companion—not merely a meeting transcription or summarisation tool.

## 3. Goals and non-goals

### 3.1 Product goals
- Detect relevant competitor references and objections.
- Retrieve approved knowledge quickly.
- Generate concise, context-aware recommendations.
- Suggest useful discovery questions.
- Show sources for factual claims.
- Let representatives control and rate assistance.
- Validate usefulness and latency in a small pilot.

### 3.2 Business goals
- Validate a repeatable B2B SaaS use case.
- Establish buyer willingness to pilot and pay.
- Measure cost per meeting and operational feasibility.
- Identify a defensible product expansion path.

### 3.3 Non-goals for MVP
- Autonomous speaking to prospects.
- Autonomous negotiation, pricing or discount approval.
- Replacing CRM or meeting software.
- Automatic changes to CRM records.
- Cross-industry production support.
- Predictive win-rate scoring.
- Covert employee monitoring.
- Automatic model training on customer data.

## 4. Users and permissions

### 4.1 Roles

| Role | Main permissions |
|---|---|

| Knowledge Manager | Upload, update, archive and assign knowledge sources |

Role names and access policies may be simplified in the prototype, but organisation-level isolation must be implemented from the beginning.

### 4.2 Primary persona

**Account Executive**
- Runs discovery, demo and negotiation calls.
- Needs short, relevant, evidence-backed recommendations.
- Cannot spend time reading long generated responses.
- Must remain in control of what is said to the customer.

### 4.3 Secondary personas
- SDR: qualification and early objections.
- Sales Manager: coaching and consistency.
- Enablement Manager: playbook and knowledge governance.
- VP Sales/CRO: business outcomes and adoption.

## 6. Key user journeys

### 6.1 Organisation onboarding
1. Admin creates an organisation.
2. Admin invites users.
3. Admin configures basic retention and privacy settings.
4. Knowledge Manager uploads approved documents.
5. System parses and indexes the documents.
6. Admin reviews indexing status and source metadata.
7. Representatives can access only knowledge permitted for their organisation and role.

**Success condition:** A representative can retrieve an approved document through a test query, and no other organisation's content is accessible.

### 6.2 Meeting preparation (later release)
1. Representative selects a meeting or account.
2. System retrieves permitted CRM/account context.
3. System prepares relevant competitor, product and discovery information.
4. Representative reviews and edits the briefing.

### 6.3 Start a meeting
1. Representative opens the companion.
2. Representative selects a supported meeting session or simulated session.
3. The interface clearly indicates whether capture is active.
4. Required participant notice and consent steps are completed according to the integration and customer policy.
5. Transcript events begin arriving.
6. Representative can pause or stop assistance.

### 6.4 Competitor mention
1. Transcript event arrives.
2. Signal detector identifies a possible competitor mention.
3. Entity resolver checks the organisation's competitor catalogue.
4. System retrieves relevant battle-card sections.
5. Context manager selects relevant customer needs and previous statements.
6. Recommendation generator creates a concise suggestion.
7. Source and uncertainty are displayed.
8. Representative can dismiss, save or rate the card.

### 6.5 Objection
1. Transcript event suggests an objection.
2. Classifier assigns a possible category and confidence.
3. Context manager checks prior discussion.
4. System retrieves approved objection-handling guidance.
5. Recommendation is generated.
6. UI indicates whether the objection is explicit or inferred.
7. Representative remains responsible for the response.

### 6.6 Qualification gap
1. System tracks configured qualification fields.
2. It detects that a field has not been adequately explored.
3. It checks whether the topic has already been answered implicitly.
4. It suggests one useful question only when contextually appropriate.
5. Representative can dismiss or mark as already covered.

### 6.7 End meeting
1. Representative stops the session.
2. System finalises the transcript if permitted.
3. Meeting summary and signal log are generated when enabled.
4. Representative can review and correct the summary.
5. Retention policy is applied.
6. Feedback is retained according to organisation settings.

## 8. Recommendation contract

Every recommendation should use a structured schema similar to:

```json
{
  "type": "competitor | objection | discovery | technical_answer | buying_signal",
  "title": "Short title",
  "trigger": {
    "transcript_excerpt": "Relevant customer statement",
    "speaker": "prospect",
    "timestamp": "00:12:31"
  },
  "interpretation": "What the system believes happened",
  "certainty": "high | medium | low",
  "suggested_action": "What the representative can do next",
  "suggested_response": "Optional concise response",
  "supporting_sources": [
    {
      "document_id": "source-id",
      "title": "Approved document",
      "section": "Relevant section"
    }
  ],
  "limitations": [],
  "created_at": "ISO-8601 timestamp"
}
```

The production schema should use strict validation, enums, length limits and identifiers generated by the backend. Do not trust arbitrary LLM output as executable instructions.

### Recommendation content rules
- Keep the initial card concise.
- Separate customer facts from AI interpretation.
- Do not present a hypothesis as a fact.
- Do not invent product capabilities, pricing or competitor weaknesses.
- Do not suggest unauthorised discounts or contractual commitments.
- Show a source for factual product and competitor claims.
- If sources conflict, show the conflict or abstain.
- Never automatically send a recommendation to the customer.

## 9. Objection taxonomy

Initial categories:
1. Price / budget.
2. Timing / priority.
3. Authority / stakeholder.
4. Competition / alternative.
5. Trust / credibility.
6. Need / fit.
7. Status quo.
8. Technical / integration.
9. Security / compliance.
10. Procurement / legal.
11. Implementation / migration.
12. Unclassified.

Classification must include:
- Category.
- Evidence excerpt.
- Confidence.
- Whether explicit or inferred.
- Relevant previous context.
- Recommended response strategy, if supported.

## 10. Competitor intelligence requirements

Each organisation can configure:
- Canonical competitor name.
- Aliases and product names.
- Approved battle cards.
- Verified differentiators.
- Known limitations, with evidence and review date.
- Relevant customer segments.
- Approved discovery questions.
- Prohibited or unverified claims.

The system must not automatically scrape and treat arbitrary public content as approved sales messaging in the MVP. Public research can be a later, separately sourced feature with provenance, freshness and legal review.

|---|---|

| Availability | Pilot target 99.5% monthly, excluding planned maintenance and upstream dependencies |
| Security | TLS in transit and encryption at rest |
| Tenant isolation | Organisation-scoped database access and tests |
| Authentication | Secure session/token handling; no secrets in frontend |
| Auditability | Record administrative actions and relevant data access |

| Resilience | Retry transient provider failures with bounded backoff |
| Observability | Structured logs, metrics, traces and error reporting |
| Accessibility | Keyboard navigation and readable contrast |
| Portability | Provider adapters where practical |
| Cost | Measure transcription, LLM and storage cost per meeting |

Targets are initial engineering goals and must be revised after a technical spike.

## 13. Privacy, security and governance

The product processes potentially sensitive customer and business conversations. The MVP must not treat privacy as a later enhancement.

Requirements:
- Clear meeting capture indicator.
- Appropriate notice and consent based on applicable law, platform terms and customer policy.
- Organisation-level configuration.
- Role-based access.
- Least-privilege access to documents and meetings.
- Encryption in transit and at rest.
- Secret management outside source control.
- Configurable transcript retention.
- Deletion workflow for transcripts and derived records.
- No use of customer data for general model training without explicit authorisation.
- Provider data-processing and retention terms reviewed before production use.
- Data residency and transfer requirements reviewed for each customer.
- Redaction or minimisation for sensitive information where appropriate.
- Audit trail for admin and data access.
- Document-level permission metadata carried into retrieval.

A recommendation must never reveal content that the representative is not authorised to access.

## 15. Analytics and success metrics

### North Star Metric
Weekly sales conversations with at least one recommendation explicitly rated helpful or confirmed as used.

Report the following separately:
- Helpful recommendations.
- Recommendations used.
- Recommendations viewed.
- Recommendations dismissed.
- Recommendations rated incorrect.

### Product metrics
| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Activation | New user completes setup and runs a first meeting session |
| Meeting activation | Eligible meetings in which the companion is used |
| Detection precision | True positive detections / all positive detections |
| Detection recall | True positive detections / all actual positive events in labelled data |
| Helpful rate | Helpful ratings / all submitted ratings |
| Usage rate | Recommendations explicitly marked used / recommendations shown |
| Duplicate rate | Repeated equivalent recommendations within cooldown |
| Latency | Time from completed trigger utterance to visible card |
| Source coverage | Factual recommendations with valid supporting sources |
| Cost per meeting | Transcription + inference + storage + processing cost |

### Pilot hypotheses
- Competitor detection precision ≥90% on a labelled evaluation set.
- Objection detection precision ≥85% on a labelled evaluation set.
- Helpful recommendation rate ≥70% among explicitly rated recommendations.
- P95 recommendation latency <5 seconds after a completed trigger utterance.
- No unsupported critical product claim in the release evaluation set.

Always report sample size and confidence intervals where practical. A small pilot does not establish revenue impact.

## 16. Acceptance test scenarios

### Scenario 1: Known competitor
**Given** the organisation has configured a competitor and uploaded an approved battle card  
**When** the prospect mentions the competitor  
**Then** the system identifies it, retrieves relevant content, displays a source and suggests a discovery question.

### Scenario 2: Unknown competitor
**Given** the prospect mentions a name not in the competitor catalogue  
**When** the detector is uncertain  
**Then** the system asks for clarification or shows an unconfirmed entity rather than inventing a battle card.

### Scenario 3: Pricing objection with context
**Given** the prospect previously discussed business impact  
**When** the prospect says the product is too expensive  
**Then** the recommendation uses relevant prior context and approved pricing guidance.

### Scenario 4: Ambiguous hesitation
**Given** the prospect says “I need time to think”  
**When** the system classifies the statement  
**Then** it labels the interpretation as uncertain and does not assume lack of interest.

### Scenario 5: Missing evidence
**Given** the prospect asks about a technical feature not documented in the knowledge base  
**When** retrieval finds no reliable source  
**Then** the system abstains and recommends confirming the answer.

### Scenario 6: Duplicate trigger
**Given** the same competitor is repeated several times  
**When** equivalent signals occur within the cooldown window  
**Then** the UI does not show repetitive cards.

### Scenario 7: Tenant isolation
**Given** two organisations have different documents  
**When** a user from organisation A searches  
**Then** no document or chunk from organisation B is returned.

### Scenario 8: Pause capture
**Given** a meeting session is active  
**When** the representative pauses it  
**Then** transcript ingestion and recommendation generation stop according to the documented pause semantics, and the UI indicates paused state.

### Scenario 9: Source deletion
**Given** a knowledge source is deleted  
**When** retrieval is performed after deletion is complete  
**Then** its chunks are not returned, and deletion propagation is logged.

### Scenario 10: Provider outage
**Given** the transcription provider becomes unavailable  
**When** the connection fails  
**Then** the UI shows a clear failure state and does not fabricate transcript content.

## 18. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Hallucinated claims | RAG, source display, strict schema and abstention |
| Noisy alerts | Confidence thresholds, cooldowns and user feedback |
| High latency | Streaming, bounded context and asynchronous processing |
| Poor transcript quality | Provider evaluation and domain vocabulary |
| Privacy issues | Consent, retention, access controls and legal review |
| Outdated documents | Owner, version, review date and expiry |
| Model/provider outage | Graceful degradation and provider abstraction |
| Cost escalation | Token limits, retrieval filters, caching and usage budgets |
| Weak adoption | Low-distraction design and user research |
| Unproven ROI | Pilot with baseline and comparison cohort |

## 21. Definition of Done for MVP

The MVP is ready for a supervised pilot when:
- Users can authenticate and are assigned to an organisation.
- Organisation isolation tests pass.
- Approved documents can be uploaded, parsed, indexed and retrieved.
- A simulated or supported live meeting can be started and stopped.
- Transcript events appear in order with timestamps.
- Competitor and objection signals are detected against a labelled test set.
- Recommendations are grounded in retrieved knowledge or explicitly abstain.
- Sources are visible and open to the permitted user.
- Duplicate recommendations are suppressed.
- Users can dismiss and rate recommendations.
- Pause/stop and capture status work correctly.
- Errors are observable and understandable.
- Privacy and retention controls are documented and tested.
- Pilot users have been trained and consent/notice requirements are met.
